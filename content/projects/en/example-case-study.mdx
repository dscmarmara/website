People queuing at the cafeteria over lunch wait 7 minutes on average, but that average hides the real question: between 12:10 and 12:40 the wait goes past 20 minutes, and after 13:30 it drops under 4. We wanted to answer a student's "if I go now, how long will I wait?" to the minute.

## Data

### What we measured

We recorded the time between passing the turnstile and leaving the till, on weekday lunchtimes over six weeks. Each record is one person's wait; the model groups them into 10-minute slots.

The baseline gives each slot the average of past weeks. LightGBM gains most in the slots when classes let out.

## Limits

<Callout tone="warning" title="What it doesn't say">

- Exam weeks are not in the data; the pattern may change then.
- The model doesn't know the menu: on a popular dish's day the queue can be longer than predicted.
- The simulated 38% assumes the cashier's speed stays the same.

</Callout>

<Details summary="Features and validation">

Each 10-minute slot used these features:

1. Time of day and day of the week
2. Number of classes ending in that slot
3. Queue length in the previous slot

Validation was done in time order: trained on the first four weeks, tested on the last two.

```python
model = lgb.LGBMRegressor(n_estimators=400, learning_rate=0.05)
model.fit(X_train, y_train)
mae = mean_absolute_error(y_test, model.predict(X_test))
```
